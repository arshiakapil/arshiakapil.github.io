The Basics: How to Think About Computing Infrastructure in Genomics

Jan 8. 2026 · Genomics

How do you choose the right computational infrastructure for a genomics problem? As sequencing data continues to grow in both size and complexity, infrastructure decisions increasingly shape which analyses are feasible, efficient, and cost-effective. In this post, I break down how to think about computational bottlenecks in genomics, including CPU, memory, and I/O constraints, and how these map to different computing environments such as cloud platforms, on-prem systems, and high-performance computing clusters. Using a whole-genome variant calling workflow as a concrete example, I show how understanding data movement and parallelism can guide more thoughtful infrastructure choices in modern genomics.

Why Genome Assembly Is Harder Than It Looks: A Puzzle Builder’s Guide

Dec 5. 2025 · Genomics

Genome assembly may seem simple, but it often feels like piecing together a massive puzzle with missing or nearly identical pieces. This post uses a beach-puzzle analogy to explain why some genomic regions are easy to assemble while others remain difficult. It breaks down the strengths and limitations of short-read and long-read sequencing, highlights the tradeoffs between cost and accuracy, and shows how targeted sequencing can provide highly reliable insights without reconstructing an entire genome. With real examples from Illumina and Myriad, this guide offers a clear, accessible look at how scientists choose the right sequencing strategy and why genome assembly is harder than it appears.
